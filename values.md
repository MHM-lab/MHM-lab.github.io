We are all different and imperfect: we fail, misjudge context, and occasionally violate our own standards. This is part of being human. Human fallibility is therefore both the reason values fail and the reason they are needed. Our values are guiding principles under uncertainty, not rigid rules.

## Attentive, kind, and honest communication

Communication is one of the most difficult, yet one of the most important interpersonal skills. Skillful communication can be cultivated. We try to build a lab based on attentive, kind, and honest communication. We take time to think about what it means for us individually and as a group and support each other in becoming better communicators with ourselves and each other.

## Celebrate and support each other
Science often rewards individuals more than groups. We celebrate each other's successes anyway, because none of our work would be possible without the people around us.

## Embrace diversity

We value diversity and try to minimize biases. This is not always easy, so we point out to each other, kindly, when unconscious biases, false beliefs, or other blind spots may be at play. Our goal is to achieve equality of opportunity.

## Learn from failure

We help each other learn from setbacks and accept that every new path involves trial and error. This is how learning works.

## Statement on artificial intelligence

We focus on developing systems that help humans.

We see large language models as useful tools with clear limits. Our [lab's policy on the use of LLMs](/use_LLMs/) sets out how we use them.

## Where we publish and why

We publish in machine learning, psychiatry, psychology, and neuroscience, because mental health mapping draws on all of these fields. Conferences reach the people who develop methods, and journals reach clinicians and neuroscientists. We support open science and try to balance reach, access, and cost. We also know that where a paper appears still matters for early-career researchers, and we take this into account.
